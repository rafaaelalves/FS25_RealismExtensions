## 1. Implementation inventory / actual ownership

| Module | Main behavior |
| --- | --- |
| `MapFlood.lua` | Map lifecycle; global field geometry; weather bucket model; terrain sampling; water-plane shapes/collisions; multiplayer state persistence and UI; global FillUnit hook. |
| `MapFloodZones.lua` | Coarse map-grid D8 flow accumulation; priority-flood spill heights; road sag candidate discovery; zone building; geometry healing and tiled meshes; server volume dynamics; pumps; traffic blockers and waves. |
| `MapFloodGeometry.lua` | Pure Lua elevation sorting/prefix sum, volume/level inverse, shape polygon/raster contours and Douglas–Peucker simplification. |
| `MapFloodPumps.lua` | Pump classification, hooks for `PowerConsumer` and `VehicleMotor`, sound, per-liter payment and HUD. |
| `MapFloodJobs.lua` | Flood mission definition/generation, scenario/difficulty/fees, legacy sandbag mission save compatibility, extra hotspot and mission lifecycle. |
| `MapFloodTankWater.lua` | Client visual water-in-container rendering; slosh and lifetime. |
| 7 `scripts/events/*.lua` | State, settings, zones, pump requests, reset, notifications and job collection. |
| GUI XML/Lua | Menu settings, flood missions and completion dialog. |

**Not a native terrain writer:** no `setTerrainHeightAtWorldPos`, `TerrainDeformation` or permanent rut-geometry modification. Floodwater is simulated by **generated plane shapes with `CollisionFlag.WATER` and selected `shallowWaterSimulation` geometry**, which can affect interaction with water. Explicitly distinguishes water volumes from wheel sink / terrain material transport.

### Terrain spatial model

- On map setup `startGridSetup` samples terrain heights into `gridH`. `GRID_MAX_CELLS=512` per axis; nominal grid pitch is max(4 m, terrainSize/512). Thus ~4 m at 2048 m map, ~8 m at 4096 m, ~16 m at 8192 m. **This is too coarse for individual tire ruts**. Max spatial samples on large maps: 512²=262,144, done in batches of 6,000 cells/frame.
- Server derives D8-like flow accumulation: heights binned at 2 cm; move each accumulation to a downstream equal/lower neighbor in rank order; sample AI traffic-road splines every 4 m, find local sags; run 4-neighbor priority-flood from boundary and existing water to find closed depressions/spill elevations; create roads and natural hollows (up to 700 zones) and tile their contours. This is meaningful basin-analysis architecture, but the coarse raster, rank tie-breaking and static world snapshot mean **not high-resolution flow hydraulics or continuously updated water routing**.
- `MapFloodGeometry.createTub` records sorted sample heights and prefix sums. `volumeAtLevel` computes sampled displacement volume and `levelForVolume` inverts it. Isolated tests PASS for heights {0,1,2} with 4 m² each: volume 8 m³ corresponds to level 1.5 m; additional histogram inversion checks passed. This correctly supplies a *single horizontal level* for a static sampled basin; it is different from the terrain-following water sheet of WIT.

### Hydrology and gameplay

- Server `hydroStep`: simplified soil **bucket reservoir** with rain/snow accumulation and melt, evaporation, percolation and rainfall overflow, per-field independent `storage`/ponded states, and a global catchment storage. Uses game time for hydrology.
- **70% field saturation trigger refers to MapFlood's *own calculated field storage*, not MudSystemPhysics local physical wetness**; bootstraps from vanilla global `weather:getGroundWetness` if there is no saved state. Runoff contribution depends on saturation and `mode.runoffFactor`. Flood zones store actual per-zone m³ and calculate elevation via inverse bath-tub volume.
- Four severity profiles govern soil storage, rates, basin scale and depth. Pumping volumes alter zone volume; flooding drains through empirical exponential time constants and infiltration. These are defensible game models **but not a validated Darcy/Richards, Saint-Venant or physically calibrated runoff solver**. Water added by unlimited reverse pumps is a gameplay source, not conservation of catchment mass.
- Generated flood surfaces, collisions, traffic-blocking map obstacles, sounds, water-meter HUD and jobs are ancillary. The mod also adds `WATER` to allowed FillUnit fill types for many globally recognized liquid machines; this mutates unrelated vehicle capabilities, not just flood behavior.
- Pump extraction/delivery, reverse pumping and mission payments use local vehicle properties. Pump rates mix **game seconds** for unlimited/fire pumps and **real seconds** for tanks; varying game time scale changes the relative throughput (MapFloodZones.lua `updatePumping`).
- Savegame `mapFlood.xml`: settings, soil/catch storage, snow pack, zone volume keyed by `startIdx`, field values and job metadata; server state/zones are networked through custom events.

## 2. Cross-stack concerns (source evidence and severity)

### MF-01 HIGH — independent soil-moisture authority

`MapFlood:hydroStep` maintains its own per-field and global storage and `MapFlood:getFieldSaturation` gates rainwater runoff. It does **not** consume RC's normalized wheel wetness nor Mud's local wetness grid. WIT instead chooses RWSM heuristics or vanilla weather. Three percentages may disagree for the *same terrain* while controlling sink, puddles and floods. Separate physical ground wetness, hydrologic soil water, agronomic moisture, and ponded water explicitly. **Do not** indiscriminately use the Mud traction coefficient as catchment saturation, nor copy MapFlood's bucket into RE as another owner. An authoritative, versioned soil/hydrology domain may be needed if actual unification is desired.

### MF-02 HIGH — static terrain snapshot after dynamic construction or ruts

`gridH`, flow routing, spill elevations, catchment, zone histograms and polygons are calculated during initial map setup. No source observer to RE `TerrainWriter`, TerraFarm or native landscaping, and no general terrain-change invalidation/recomputation. Water and collision planes can remain based on stale geometry when terrain changes; saved zone volumes keyed by initial grid `startIdx` are not sufficient to migrate across geometry/heightmap changes. **Source-confirmed contract absence, not reproduced visual failure in-game**. On 4096 m map, 8 m raster may wholly miss RE tire ruts: do not promise that MapFlood fills RE rut geometry.

### MF-03 HIGH — three global PTO/engine hooks overlap RC/RE ownership

`MapFlood.installPumpLoadHooks` wraps **`PowerConsumer.getMaxPtoRpm`**, **`PowerConsumer.getTotalConsumedPtoTorque`**, and **`VehicleMotor.getRequiredMotorRpmRange`**. Pumping marks the towing/root motor. It adds a fixed +500 rpm governor request and +0.5 kNm torque. At 540 shaft rpm, 0.5 kNm ≈28.3 kW, **but measured pump flow, head/pressure, shaft mode/ratio, efficiency, drive type and hydraulic/electric origin are not accounted for**. The comment says wrapper is installed *after* other mods, but late overwrite placement is not evidence of correct semantics or execution when RC/native RE PTO also owns the ratio and MR/RMS motor paths. This is a **direct shared method/writer risk** and warrants an isolated RE PTO A/B before any stack admission. No blanket RC bridge until a reproduced pair/version-specific conflict; a long-term dedicated pump-workload contract should feed canonical motor/implement owners.

### MF-04 HIGH — pump MP event missing caller vehicle control/ownership check

`MapFloodPumpEvent:run(connection)` (file lines 44–66) on client→server validates vehicle existence/class (water fill unit or emergency), then invokes `setVehiclePumping`, **without checking whether the requesting connection controls or is authorized for that vehicle/farm**. Isolated Lua harness with mocked connection and a valid differently owned vehicle shows handler accepts the request. This verifies the missing check, **not a complete network exploit**; server runtime/permission pathway must be tested. Settings/reset events *do* guard master user; job collection checks farm. Never copy pump-event authorization.

### MF-05 HIGH — advertised external intake-volume accounting is unreachable in supplied Lua

`MapFlood:onExternalWaterIntake(vehicle, liters)` is defined in `MapFloodZones.lua` (~1922) and designed to subtract third-party intake from flood zones. **No call site to this function appears anywhere among the ZIP's Lua**, and `MapFlood.ownFillActive` is set/cleared by `addOwnFillLevel` but not consulted by a registered intake hook. Although `FillUnit.onLoad` is extended to allow water types, that does not itself report or deduct flood intake volume. **Source-level unconnected pathway:** do not certify the BETA 2 claim that every normal tank/pump intake reduces flooded volume; native integration from outside the supplied Lua could exist, but none was identified.

### MF-06 MEDIUM/HIGH — all-liquid-FillUnit global modification

`FillUnit.onLoad` is globally appended; `extendLiquidFillUnits` edits `fillUnit.supportedFillTypes` on any eligible vehicle with LIQUID/SLURRYTANK/SPRAYER-like types (excluding recognized fuel). Adds `WATER` to far more machinery than just actively pumping objects. Potential side effects to sprayer/carrier/contract rules and other mods; not verified in-game. Not removed on MapFlood teardown. Prefer opt-in machine capability rather than broad type mutation in any independent replacement.

### MF-07 MEDIUM/HIGH — mixed time bases, cap/energy inconsistency

`updatePumping` calculates `gameSeconds=step * timeScale /1000` and `realSeconds=step/1000`; unlimited pumps remove/add at game-time rates while tanks use real-time rates. Under x1 vs x120 time acceleration, an otherwise comparable pump's effectiveness changes enormously relative to weather, hydrological inflow and other pumps. Stable simulation timebase should be chosen and documented. Fixed engine load independent of actual liters/sec further breaks input/output causality.

### MF-08 MEDIUM/HIGH — performance and geometry budgets

Positive: setup is staged; 6,000 terrain samples/frame, 400 scan rays/frame, ~6,000 priority-flood cells/frame, limited zone meshing, some camera-radius SWS resources, 3,000 ms traffic update. However, wide-map persistent data includes `gridH`, D8 arrays, priority floods, region masks, sorted elevation arrays and potentially **700 multi-shape zones**, mirrored water surfaces, collision shape rebuilds and raycast-based water detection. A few worker paths can run unbounded inside a per-frame step: `stepDepressionFill` drains an entire connected plate in its flood loop even after its `scanBudget` counter falls negative; `stepZonePlanes` fallback uses `stepHealJob(job, math.huge)` until done. These are **source-visible frame hitch risks**, not measured FPS regressions. Need perf instrumentation and map-size scaling.

### MF-09 MEDIUM — save/load + MP state contracts incomplete for verification

Unlike WIT, MapFlood has meaningful authoritative server hydrology, custom zone/state events and serialized persistent volumes. This is a strong engineering pattern. Risks: client reconstructs a zone from its own initial height raster and server start indices/levels, assuming exact client/server heights; save links volumes to `startIdx`, not immutable basin ID/geometry revision; no surfaced heightmap-epoch check. MP live JIP after map modification, late zone rebuild, mission payouts and save compatibility untested. Manifest `multiplayer supported=true` is not proof of release-readiness.

### MF-10 MEDIUM — shared shallow-water and rendering responsibilities with WIT

MapFlood allocates polygonal water meshes/collision and adds selected geometry to `g_currentMission.shallowWaterSimulation`; WIT creates separate rut-layer triangles and registers selected shapes into the same SWS. The two packages are not in an intentional shared hydrology contract, do not share water heights/volumes, and can double-overlay the same depression, duplicate SWS demand or present inconsistent floodwater. **Not proof of a crash**. First test each independently; do not stack automatically.

### MF-11 MEDIUM — mission/contract ownership (not just water)

`MapFloodJobs.lua` registers `FloodMission` and inert historical `SandbagMission`, uses mission-manager registration/hotspots and pays for pumped volumes. MapFlood additionally generates artificial flood jobs in dry regions. This potentially touches the user's contracts/mission stack independently of physical water; do not add an RC bridge merely because a mod registers missions. If testing, start with jobs disabled; only evaluate job integration after water stability, and test multiple farm ownership contexts.

## 3. Relative quality: MapFlood vs Water In Deep Tire Tracks vs RE

| Mechanism | MapFlood 10 | WIT 0.9.1.5 | Current RE / RC |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Heightmap writer | None | None | **RE TerrainWriter** (experimental; native brush jobs) |
| Spatial scale | **Map-wide ≤512² grid**; zones/fields | **6 m tile mesh**, wheel-local activation | Per-wheel footprint / spatial rut histories |
| Water level | **Horizontal volume-derived basin elevation** | Sloping reference sheet minus moisture-driven offset | No water-surface owner |
| Water source | **Own** field/global bucket, rain and snow melt | RWSM heuristic → vanilla weather | Mud physical wetness via RC; agronomic water other owner |
| Flow/hydrology | D8 catchment + priority-flood + empirical inflow/drain | Not a hydrologic flow solver | Rut deformation/mass movement of soil, not flowing water |
| Mechanical impact | Water collision + pumping torque/RPM hooks; traffic blockers | Shallow-water triangles + visual player-offset | MR/Mud/RMS/RE PTO own mechanical response |
| Multiplayer | Dedicated server logic, state/zones/pump events, farm-job model | Primarily client-local presentation | RE MP deferred; RC gated integrations |
| Provenance after terrain editing | **No explicit terrain-epoch invalidation** | Cached reference never automatically rebuilt | SpatialHistory and TerrainPersistence need cross-writer reconciliation |

**Why not copy directly:** MapFlood owns a meaningful different-scale hydrologic phenomenon; in principle a **real Hydrology/SurfaceWater owner** could complement RE geometry, but its current wetness duplication, mechanics hooks, desynced terrain snapshot and authorization gaps prevent immediate stack adoption. A full functional reimplementation would be substantial and not justified solely by mod count. WIT contains useful near-wheel surface presentation but no mass-conserving or horizontal-basin solver.

## 4. Engineering opportunities to preserve (not implementation approvals)

1. **Hydrology contract independent of rut simulation**: `TerrainElevation` (versioned surface/epoch), `Hydrology` (basin ID, current storage m³, spill height, catchment volume, inflow/outflow), `SurfaceWaterPresentation` (render-only + optional SWS shapes), `PumpWorkload` (flow, head, power source, measured work), `RC/RE physical contact` (Mud authoritative wheel sink/wetness and MR drivetrain).
2. **Volume↔level geometry** is a good clean-room design candidate for a future **persistent rut/puddle storage** feature. At rut scale, however, use high-resolution geomorphology tied to RE local pre-rut reference; MapFlood's coarse grid will not resolve typical ruts.
3. **Surface mutation provenance** (same lesson as Auto Leveler). Recompute or rebase basin geometry after verified RE/TerraFarm/landscape edits. Preserve unrelated water mass; consider overflow to neighbors when a basin is filled or removed. Avoid blind recomputation per wheel brush.
4. **Physical pumps integrated through owner contracts**, not three unrelated power/ratio hooks. The input may be a hydraulically driven, PTO-driven, engine-driven or electric pump; real power ∼ density×gravity×flow×head / efficiency (plus appropriate line losses). Simulate demand only where GIANTS/MR/RMS need an explicit missing source; recognize RE PTO and AI control semantics.
5. **Demand-driven hierarchical grids**: coarse catchment maps computed once for a stable terrain region, local refined 0.25–1 m or wheel footprint if justified, dirty region/epoch cache invalidation rather than solving global hydro at per-wheel cadence.
6. **Gameplay opportunity**: roads cut by floods, real pumping/logistics, scheduled repairs and construction, subject to eventual project planning. Do not conflate this with a mandate to absorb the mod into RE now.

## 6. Recommended investigation and explicit decision

**No production-stack install at present**, especially **not alongside WIT** on primary save. **No automatic bridge, no new RC version gate, no RE implementation or build** from this audit.

If optional isolated tests are desired later: (1) backup/save with MapFlood alone, jobs disabled, observe hydrology under rain and `mfZones`; (2) test terrain mutation and save reload after zone creation, comparing zone level and geometry; (3) test standalone pump/tank, intake accounting and high time-scale discrepancy; (4) controlled RC+MR+RE PTO A/B on disposable save with pumping and owner-specific telemetry, before other mods; (5) solo MapFlood vs WIT SWS/visual concurrency; (6) dedicated MP user/vehicle authorization and late join; (7) 2 km/4 km maps frame-time, zone mesh count, memory and map-reload leak test. Explicitly keep the **RE rut recovery V3 acceptance** and native PTO evolution as independent existing priorities.

*Incorporation verdict:* **high research value** for watershed routing, basin storage, pump logistics and contracts; **low confidence of safe plug-and-play compatibility**, **NO functional absorption approval** until we choose which specific capability is genuinely missing and can be owned coherently.

## 7. Decision refinement — **isolated experimental-stack trial warranted** (2026-10-25)

Reassessing source risks against the intended ability to experiment with promising external specialists: **MapFlood is sufficiently different from RE to merit a separate disposable-save A/B**, rather than indefinite research-only shelving. The source audit does **not** certify production compatibility or recommend installing WIT simultaneously. The preferred first experiment tests *flood hydrology and water presentation*, not pump mechanical integrations or mission economy.

**Source-supported menu profile** from `MapFlood.OPTIONS` (`scripts/MapFlood.lua` lines 30–38):
- `enabled = true`, `strength = 1 (LOW)`, `puddles = true`;
- `pumping = false`, `jobs = false`; HUD and alarms optional.
- Use a cloned save on the target map and start with MapFlood as the only new water mod. Observe initial terrain analysis, `mfZones`, rain response, low roads, shallow-water interactions, save/reload, frame-time and logs.

**Important source caveat:** setting `pumping = false` does **NOT** uninstall or skip the three global pump wrappers: `MapFlood.installPumpLoadHooks()` is called unconditionally on `loadMap` (line ~365). They should normally pass through their `superFunc` while `pumpRpmRoots` remains empty, but retained function wrappers still complicate MR/RE PTO hook identity/order. Moreover, a global `FillUnit.onLoad` append can widen `WATER` fill-type eligibility regardless of the pump menu setting. Observe `rcHooks`, power/PTO behaviors, save transitions, and do not equate "pumping off" to isolation of *all* MapFlood hooks. A source-level or runtime owner conflict still warrants failing closed and leaving MapFlood out of the primary stack.

**Promotion criteria:** repeat success with RC/RE and existing specialists, no regressions in normal PTO/fill units, acceptable setup/scene frame-time and water geometry, no loss of progress/save correctness. Pumping and missions stay off until independently audited/validated. If the hydrologic gameplay value is high, favor keeping an external MapFlood owner and building a narrow, versioned compatibility contract **only where measured**. Absorbing D8/priority-flood and volume-elevation *ideas* into future RE functionality is optional; copy neither source nor assets.

This is a revised **testing/admission recommendation**, not a code implementation, merge, build, final enablement decision or reversal of the source findings.
