# Mud 1.4.0.0 — owner contract for surface water, geometry and RE terrain persistence

**Date:** 2026-11-07. **Source:** exact user-provided Mud 1.4.0.0 ZIP (SHA-256 `3f004b7904b85e1c860bd47be62ff8bcb95830a5ca478088b823588f4dfbdf47`), compared with exact Mud 1.3.9.0 archive. Full source-level inventory, per-bridge contract and read-only Lua checks are in [RC Mud 1.4 stacked PR #52](https://github.com/rafaaelalves/FS25_RealismCompatibility/pull/52). This note is a **research-only RE architecture clarification**, not a runtime feature change or an endorsement to install two active water owners.

## 1. Four different ground domains must not be confused

| Ground concept | Current owner | Mutates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Instantaneous traction/contact/sink** | Mud + MR + Reifen under RC composition | Wheel physics, slip/load and temporary effective radius, not native terrain height |
| **Persistent rut & earth displacement** | RE native `TerrainWriter` / `SpatialHistory` | Heightmap; requires actual native callback confirmation, including asynchronous jobs |
| **Surface classification/crop density** | GIANTS native plus Mud 1.3.9 `TurfDamageSystem` | `FieldDensityMap.GROUND_TYPE`, grass/MEADOW crop density; not heightmap |
| **Surface water/ice storage** | **Mud 1.4 `LocalPuddleSystem`** | Per-basin volumes and ice in `mudSystemPuddles.xml`; dynamic wheel wetness/sink, rice-water visual contour sheets and physical ice colliders; not heightmap |

The *same patch of soil* can be geometrically rutted, classified as cultivated, saturated, temporarily soft, flooded and frozen. These are related phenomena but neither an interchangeable scalar nor justification for separate, independently authoritative traction formulas.

## 2. What the new Mud actually knows about the landscape

The exact new `FieldLocalWetnessLayer` requests 4m hydrology cells but caps its square grid at **256×256**; for the user's possible ~2km map this becomes **8m/cell**, for 4× (~4km) **16m/cell**. Cell construction looks at high-res terrain minima within each coarse cell; real visual shorelines are sampled more densely, but the *volume/topological basin* remains coarse.

A new `LocalPuddleSystem` priority-flood finds depression outlets and connected basins on first hydrology scan, and uses pooled water levels, runoff, evaporation/infiltration and snowmelt. It has a **minimum basin depression 0.06m** and water depth cap 0.65m. It performs staged/time-budgeted updates and persists state by basin id, floor and spill level.

**Important mismatch:** `LocalPuddleSystem:startTopology` runs when `sourceCells` changes; RE terrain height edits **do not notify it**. A narrow 10cm-deep rut written after a Mud topology scan is therefore **not guaranteed to create a new pool**. A later manual/full Mud rescan can rebuild topology, but the previous basin restore validates floor and spill within **0.03m**; a geometry change can invalidate and discard old basin water volume. It is misleading to say that Mud 1.4 now automatically fills RE ruts.

Mud's surface sheets use native rice water material/contours, but **not a native TerrainDeformation height writer**; ice uses collision-aware planes near vehicles and players and can change actual contact support. RE must not assume wheel loads, collision heights or rut depth is unaffected by new ice.

## 3. What an eventual minimal integration would require

No implementation now. If a game A/B proves the gap matters:

1. RE signals **completed, actually applied** height changes only after the TerrainDeformation callback confirms native commit (never at enqueue), ideally through a **read-only event** `TerrainGeometryChanged{bbox,epoch,heightDeltaRange,saveId}`.
2. Mud/RC consumes it **only if the Mud source exposes a stable, bounded per-region basin invalidation API**. A broad `startTerrainScan` after every wheel would be enormously expensive and may reset reservoir volumes.
3. A versioned basin remapping/rebalance contract must conserve **surface water and soil moisture** when floor/spill geometry changes. No RE-owned duplicate water/ice solver, no direct edit of Mud private basins or erosion-by-ad-hoc brushes.
4. Keep physics, visual and save concerns separate: wet wheel grip remains Mud/MR, physical ice remains Mud if adopted, terrain geometry remains RE, native seasonal weather is input source, and GRLE/puddle XML snapshots must agree across save/reload/client join.

**Run-time decision remains separate:** source-only PR #52 adds exact Mud 1.4 source gates and Reifen wrapper recognition but does not prove this new behavior on RE terrain.

## 4. Three inexpensive experiments

- **A — baseline:** Mud 1.4 with puddles **OFF**, RE terrain ON, same wet/dry field; compare previous Mud 1.3.9 rut depth and recovery with 1.4's faster soil drying and unchanged Reifen/pressure APIs, avoiding water as an extra variable.
- **B — water alone:** RE terrain OFF, puddles ON; a known existing depression after rain, then drying, save/reload, native water proximity, one asphalt vs soft-soil wheel; observe basin depth, wheel grip, drift and CPU on target map. Compare local-wetness ON vs OFF simplified puddles.
- **C — actual collaboration need:** with a stable already-filled basin and subsequent RE rut **after Mud initial scan**, inspect whether puddle topology follows the new height, then (in disposable save) trigger a manual Mud wetness scan and examine changes in basin identity/volume. This is the concrete evidence needed before designing any bridge.

**Winter scope optional:** ice lid after cold hold, static collision and snowmelt on a small vehicle; do not block non-winter releases on theoretical ice performance, but preserve unverified status.

**Important practical changes:** New Mud shared preferences migrate from save-local XML to a profile-global `modSettings/FS25_mudSystemPhysics/settings.xml`; keep old settings backup. Mud now warns about MapFlood/RutFlow/RealTireWear, and the author explicitly discourages stacking other flood systems. Prefer one surface-water authority; MapFlood, Water in Deep Tire Tracks and RutFlow remain separate audited alternatives.

No RC/RE behavior change, no build, no private third-party source copied. This research should not expand the ongoing terrain clipping/deformation task unless the user decides its hydrology support is worth a dedicated experimental line.
